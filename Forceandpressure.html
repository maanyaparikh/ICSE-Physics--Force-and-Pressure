Force & Pressure

Understand the chapter from the basics: force, effects of force, contact and non-contact forces, pressure, atmospheric pressure, and everyday applications.

1. Chapter at a glance

Force

A force is a push or pull that can change the state of motion, direction, or shape of an object.

SI unit

Force is measured in newton (N).

Pressure

Pressure is the force acting normally on a unit area of a surface.

SI unit

Pressure is measured in pascal (Pa).

2. What is force?

When you push a door, pull a drawer, kick a ball, or lift a bag, you are applying a force.

→
BOX

Remember: Force needs an interaction. A force may be a push or a pull.

Effects of force

Start motion

A stationary football can start moving when kicked.

Stop motion

Brakes apply force to slow and stop a bicycle.

Change direction

A bat can change the direction of a moving ball.

Change shape

Squeezing a sponge changes its shape.

3. Types of forces

Contact forces

These act only when objects are in contact.

  • Muscular force: force exerted by muscles.
  • Frictional force: force that opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact.

Non-contact forces

These can act without direct physical contact.

  • Gravitational force
  • Magnetic force
  • Electrostatic force

4. Pressure

Pressure tells us how concentrated a force is over an area.

Pressure = Force ÷ Area    →    P = F/A

SI unit: pascal (Pa), where 1 Pa = 1 N/m².

For the same force: smaller area → greater pressure
For the same area: greater force → greater pressure

Sharp knife

A sharp edge has a smaller area, so the same force produces greater pressure.

School bag straps

Broad straps spread the force over a larger area, reducing pressure on the shoulders.

High heels

A smaller contact area produces greater pressure than a broad sole for the same force.

Camels

Broad feet spread their weight over a larger area, helping them walk on sand.

5. Atmospheric pressure

Air has weight and therefore exerts pressure. The pressure exerted by the air surrounding Earth is called atmospheric pressure.

At sea level, standard atmospheric pressure ≈ 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa

Why don't we feel crushed by atmospheric pressure?

The pressure inside our bodies balances atmospheric pressure to a large extent. So the forces are not simply acting from outside without opposition.

Straw

When you suck air from a straw, the pressure inside the straw decreases. Atmospheric pressure on the liquid surface helps push liquid up the straw.

Suction cup

Removing much of the air from beneath a suction cup creates lower pressure there; atmospheric pressure helps hold the cup against the surface.

Barometer

A barometer is an instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.

6. Solved examples

Example 1 — Find pressure

A force of 60 N acts normally on an area of 3 m². Find the pressure.

P = F/A = 60/3 = 20 Pa

Example 2 — Find force

A pressure of 50 Pa acts on an area of 4 m². Find the force.

F = P × A = 50 × 4 = 200 N

Example 3 — Concept check

Why does a nail have a pointed tip?

The pointed tip has a very small area. For the same applied force, smaller area gives greater pressure, making it easier to enter the material.

7. Quick ICSE-style quiz

Q1. Which is a non-contact force?

Answer: C — Magnetic force.

Q2. What happens to pressure if area decreases while force stays the same?

Answer: A — Pressure increases.

Q3. What is the SI unit of pressure?

Answer: B — Pascal (Pa).

Q4. A force of 100 N acts on 5 m². Find pressure.

Answer: A — 20 Pa.
